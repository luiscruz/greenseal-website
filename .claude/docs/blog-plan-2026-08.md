## TS 20125 deep-dive expansion (posts 3, 4, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13)

These eight were scoped from a longer candidate list, vetted by green-software-expert for technical substance against the actual standard (PDF at `reference/iso-iec-ts-20125-1-2026.pdf`, not just secondary summaries). Three candidates were deliberately left out of this plan rather than forced in:

- **Maintenance phase deep dive** — too thin alone (2 of its 4 ecopractices just restate the Operations phase). If revisited, frame narrowly as "the standard's one training clause vs. GSP's whole Training & Knowledge category," not a full phase piece.
- **Annex A (applicability by service type)** — real content but a reference table, prose around it just restates rows. Better as an interactive checklist or downloadable than a blog post.
- **A comprehensive "every ecopractice" pillar post** — don't schedule as a dated post, it would cannibalize posts 1, 3, 4, and 8. Build it later as an evergreen capstone/index page that links back to the phase deep-dives, once they exist.
- **A "Technical Specification vs. International Standard" piece** was also considered and dropped from this round: the review-cycle rule under ISO/IEC Directives is citable, but forecasting what happens to TS 20125 specifically (revision, promotion to IS, timeline) isn't something GreenSeal can verify or should imply.

**Copyright note, applies to posts 9, 10, 11, 13 especially** (the annex-heavy ones): paraphrase requirements and describe table contents in our own words rather than reproducing ISO/IEC tables or verbatim clause text. Citing clause/annex numbers and the standard's name is fine; recreating tables (Annex C's impact categories, Annex D's methodology list, Annex B's tier matrix) or quoting extended passages verbatim is not, per ISO's own copyright policy. Illustrate with a couple of representative items instead of the complete table. Get this checked against ISO's licensing terms (iso.org) or legal counsel before this expansion goes further, given the volume of annex-derived content now planned.

**Source PDF provenance, confirmed during post 3's technical review (2026-07-31):** `reference/iso-iec-ts-20125-1-2026.pdf` is not the final published text. Its own title page identifies it as `ISO_IEC CD TS 20125_annotated by ISO/CS`, WG4 document N 121, dated 2025-07-21/23, placeholder-titled `ISO/IEC TS 20125-#:202x(X)`, with "Expected action: DTS" (meaning Draft Technical Specification was still the *next* stage after this one, at least one full ballot cycle before the Feb 2026 publication). It carries a 2025 copyright footer throughout and unresolved editorial artifacts (doubled words like "optimiseoptimize", stray capitalization from redlines, and renumbering brackets from 6.3.6 onward, e.g. "6.3.6[6.4.1]", where Stage 4 clauses were being folded into Stage 3's numbering). Clauses 6.3.1-6.3.5 (Stage 3: Implementation, used in post 3) are NOT affected by that renumbering and read as structurally stable. No publicly accessible source (iso.org included, 403s to automated fetches; public writeups don't go clause-by-clause) currently lets us confirm the exact final wording or numeric figures (compression levels, resolutions, thresholds) without a purchased copy of the final 2026 edition. Treat this PDF as directionally reliable for structure (which ecopractices exist, the shall/should split, general clause organization) but hedge or spot-check any post that leans on a *specific number* pulled from it, especially posts 4, 6, and the annex posts (9, 10, 11, 13), which cite more numeric detail than post 3 did. If anyone on the team has access to the actual purchased 2026 edition (Luis's WG4/NEN involvement, see post 14, may make this straightforward), a one-time pass to confirm the numbers already published (post 3's codec figures) and to replace this reference PDF for future posts would remove this caveat for the rest of the series.
